# Scope and limitations

The main note references the principal absorption and existence results of the two sources instead of reprinting their development. Its proof case studies concern reserve matching, safe compatible decomposition and the deduction of compatible absorbers. Its derived statements are completion avoiding a partial system, exact intersections, an initial polynomial intersection interval and polynomial excess in partial parallel classes or triangle rounds.

The source article's selected endpoints are 1.7, 1.11, 6.1, 2.8, 6.2 (with an additive ambient-old-vertex interface) and 6.3. The survey selection includes efficient refined omni-absorbers, full fixed-index design existence, its Steiner specialization, a counterexample to the literal global embedding conclusion, and a separately proved piecewise variant. This is selective formalization, not certification of every statement or application of either text. General high-girth claims are outside the established scope.

The full existence endpoint allows every fixed index at least one, for fixed q > r ≥ 2, at all sufficiently large orders admissible for that index. The finite block family has no repetitions. No single threshold uniform in the index is asserted.

In completion, sparsity is measured in the covered r-edge graph at (r−1)-sets. The forbidden partial system is arbitrary. The additional crossing bound only concerns forbidden blocks wholly in the residual graph. Exact-intersection embedding chooses an injection with the two systems at every sufficiently large admissible order. The spectrum result chooses both systems and supplies an initial interval; it does not give the complete spectrum or every prescribed first system.

The polynomial error counts excess classes/rounds. It does not bound the uncovered vertices in each class and does not assert a resolution into spanning factors. Nash–Williams uses exactly 3n ≤ 4δ(G), with no near-regularity premise. Unused colour labels are permitted. The quantitative colouring input retains its edge-count cap and is attributed through Ehard–Glock–Joos; the exact polynomial contract is not attributed verbatim to Pippenger–Spencer (1989).

The ambient absorber interface forbids edges wholly in the entire old vertex set, including isolated labels; mixed old/new edges are permitted. The survey's global and piecewise embedding predicates are not identified. Its cited Appendix B proof exists; the perfect-matching obstruction concerns the stated global conclusion.

Classical Steiner triple-system intersections, including Lindner–Rosa and its corrigendum, are acknowledged. No historical priority for every general-parameter consequence is claimed. Internal agent review, local compilation, official Comparator/kernel checks and registry review have separate evidentiary scopes.
